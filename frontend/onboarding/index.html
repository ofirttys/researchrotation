    Who we are: the REI Division

    The Division of Reproductive Endocrinology and Infertility is part of the University of Toronto Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology. Its clinical and research home is Mount Sinai Fertility (MSF) at Mount Sinai Hospital (Sinai Health), with satellite clinics in Vaughan and Mississauga.

    The division trains REI fellows, residents and students, and is one of Canada's largest academic fertility programs. We also run the Clinical Embryology program, the only one in Canada, training future embryologists.

    What we do clinically

    • Infertility assessment and treatment: ovarian reserve testing (AMH, antral follicle count), ovulation induction, IUI, IVF and ICSI.
    • Embryology and andrology labs: embryo culture, vitrification, frozen embryo transfer, time-lapse imaging, and semen analysis and processing.
    • Genetic testing: preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy (PGT-A).
    • Reproductive surgery: office and operative hysteroscopy, and sonohysterography.
    • Recurrent pregnancy loss and early pregnancy care.
    • Third-party reproduction: donor sperm and donor eggs.
    • Fertility preservation: for medical and elective indications.
    • Inclusive care: fertility care for 2SLGBTQIA+ and gender-diverse patients.

    Our partners

    To name just a few: SickKids, Women's College Hospital, Princess Margaret Cancer Centre and Sunnybrook, and the provincial and national data registries BORN Ontario and CARTR Plus. We work with many more partners across Ontario and Canada.

    Patient recruitment

    5.1 Why recruitment matters

    Every prospective study we run depends on recruitment. A study that doesn't enrol enough participants can't answer its research question, loses funding time, and wastes the effort of the patients who already took part. Many of our studies can only enrol at a specific moment in a patient's treatment, such as before stimulation starts, at the trigger, or at an early pregnancy scan. If that moment is missed, the patient usually can't be enrolled.

    Your goal is simple: no eligible patient should be missed. Two things make that happen: careful communication and steady follow-through.

    5.2 Know your studies well

    • For every study you recruit for, know the eligibility criteria, the recruitment window, and what taking part involves (visits, samples, questionnaires, reimbursement). You should be able to explain each study in two or three plain sentences.
    • Keep a one-page cheat sheet for each study with the inclusion and exclusion criteria, the timing window, and who usually refers patients.
    • Check that you're using the current REB-approved consent form and script before every contact.

    5.3 Stay visible to the people who refer patients

    Physicians, nurse practitioners, fellows and nurses identify most of our participants, but they're busy and it's easy for them to forget a study.

    • Introduce yourself to each referring clinician and tell them which studies you're recruiting for.
    • Send short, regular reminders about which studies are open and who is eligible, for example a weekly email or a quick check-in at the start of clinic.
    • Offer simple aids, such as a pocket card or a one-line eligibility summary for each study.
    • Close the loop. Let the clinician know what happened with each patient they referred (contacted, enrolled or declined). Clinicians keep referring when they can see their referrals lead somewhere.

    5.4 A daily routine that stops referrals slipping through

    • Check eIVF tasks and the MSFResearch@sinaihealth.ca mailbox at least twice a day: first thing in the morning, and again early in the afternoon.
    • Reply to every referral the same day, and immediately if the recruitment window is close.
    • Where the REB-approved protocol allows it, review upcoming clinic lists and cycle start dates to flag possibly eligible patients to their physician ahead of time. Do this only as the protocol permits and in line with PHIPA.
    • Know how weekend and after-hours recruitment is handled for each study, so that urgent patients aren't lost.

    5.5 Communicating with patients

    Many of our patients are going through fertility treatment, pregnancy loss or a cancer diagnosis. How you approach them matters as much as what you say.

    • Start warmly. Introduce yourself, say who referred them and why you're calling, and ask whether now is a good time.
    • Use plain language. Avoid jargon, and check that they've understood.
    • Listen. Let them ask questions, and acknowledge concerns without rushing past them.
    • Be clear that participation is voluntary. Tell every patient that saying no won't affect their care, and mean it.
    • Give them time to decide. Tell them when you'll follow up.
    • Stay within your role. Refer medical questions to their physician or the nursing team.

    5.6 Persistence without pressure

    • Patients often miss calls during treatment. One missed call doesn't mean they aren't interested.
    • Follow-up standard: make at least 3 attempts over about 5 business days, changing the time of day and the channel (phone, then email, then phone). Record each attempt.
    • If you still can't reach the patient, let the referring clinician know. They may be seeing the patient soon and can mention it.
    • Being persistent means making sure the patient has a chance to decide. It never means pushing them toward a yes. Once a patient declines, thank them, record it, and don't contact them again.

    5.7 Track your numbers and learn from them

    Keep a simple recruitment log for each study that records:

    • how many patients were identified, contacted, consented and enrolled;
    • why patients declined or were missed (unreachable, outside the window, ineligible, and so on).

    Review the log every week with Swati. Patterns show you where to act. For example, many "missed window" patients point to a referral timing problem, and many unreachable patients point to a need to change the contact method. Bring ideas for improvement to your progress meetings.

    5.8 Getting the basics right

    The general workflow

    1. A clinician identifies a potential participant and introduces the study.
    2. The patient agrees to be contacted.
    3. You contact them using the approved script.
    4. They review the consent form and ask questions.
    5. The consent discussion takes place and the form is signed.
    6. You enrol the participant (study ID and arm, if applicable).
    7. You document in eIVF and the study logs.
    8. You coordinate study visits and follow-up.

    For every participant, document

    The screening checklist, the consent discussion note, two signed copies of the consent form (one for Sinai's records, one for the patient), the study ID or arm, the eIVF entry, and any reimbursement receipt.

    Report any deviation or error to Swati and the PI the same day. Each study's specific steps are kept in its own SOP, which Swati will go through with you.

    REB and regulatory

    7.1 The basics

    All human-participant research here needs approval from the Sinai Health Research Ethics Board (REB) before any study activity starts, and the approval must stay current for as long as the study runs. Submissions are made through SHORES, the REB's online system.

    Each study has an REB number such as 24-0084-E: the year, a sequence number, and a letter for the review type. The letters appear to be -A (full board), -E (expedited/delegated) and -C (chart review). To be confirmed

    Quality-improvement projects go through a QI screening step to confirm they don't need full REB review.

    7.2 The study lifecycle

    StageWhat you doTips
    Initial submissionAssemble the protocol, consent forms, scripts, recruitment materials, data collection forms, budget, and team CVs and TCPS 2 certificatesUse the templates on the shared drive. The PI signs off before submission.
    REB queriesCoordinate the answers with the PI, then resubmitTrack every query in the tracker so none sits unanswered.
    AmendmentsSubmit any change (protocol, consent form, materials or sites) and get approval before putting it into practiceThe only exception is a change needed to remove an immediate risk to participants.
    Personnel changesSubmit an amendment when someone joins or leaves the study teamYour own addition to your studies will be one of your first submissions.
    Annual renewalSubmit before the approval expiresIf approval lapses, all study activity must stop, including recruitment.
    Reportable eventsReport protocol deviations, adverse events and privacy breaches within the REB's timelinesTell Swati and the PI the same day.
    Closure / terminationSubmit the closure report when the study is finishedThen move the study to the Closed sheet in the tracker.

    7.3 Special cases

    • Multi-site studies: some are reviewed through Clinical Trials Ontario (CTO). For example, in the Oncofertility AfterCare study we're a sub-site alongside Princess Margaret and SickKids. Others need approval from a second REB, such as Women's College Hospital.
    • Drug trials: these also need Health Canada authorization (Division 5) and registration on ClinicalTrials.gov.
    • Animal studies: these don't go to the REB. They follow animal-care approvals instead.

    7.4 Document version control

    • Every approved document carries a version number and date in its footer and file name, for example Protocol-V4-September 2_2025_C.
    • Submit amendments as a tracked-changes copy and a clean copy.
    • Once a new version is approved, stop using the old one straight away, file it as superseded, and let the clinicians and fellows know.
    • The tracker's "REB Approved/Submitted Documents" column must always show the current versions.

    7.5 Regulatory binder (essential documents)

    Each study keeps a paper or electronic binder containing approval letters and approved documents; correspondence with the REB; the delegation log; training records and CVs; screening and enrolment logs; the deviation log; and signed consent forms, stored securely.

    7.6 The REB MasterFile tracker

    The tracker is the team's master record of every study.

    • Active Studies: grouped by PI. Status labels include Upcoming, Almost completed, REB pending, QI, Grants, Co-PI and Animal model.
    • Closed / Inactive Studies: terminated, closed or lapsed studies, with dates and notes.
    • Finance sheet: internal orders and cost centres (Section 8).

    Key columns: study title, internal number, REB number, study type, funding and PI; renewal due dates for each year, and notes; current approved document versions; lead coordinator; important notes for coverage during absences; and task delegation.

    How to use it

    • Every month, check which renewals are due in the next 60 days.
    • Update the tracker the same day you submit anything or receive an approval.
    • Record where each open REB query stands.
    • When a study closes, file the closure report and move the study to the Closed sheet.

    Onboarding project: update and upgrade the tracker

    The current tracker needs both updating and upgrading. Work with Swati to:

    • check and update every entry: due dates, status, current document versions and lead coordinator;
    • improve how all studies are tracked on a continuous basis, so the tracker stays current without a periodic clean-up;
    • make sure no renewal is ever missed, for example with a rolling 60-day renewal view and clear status fields;
    • add an agreements log (Section 9).

    Agreements and legal

    9.1 When an agreement is needed

    Whenever a study involves another organization (sharing data, sending samples, receiving funding or study drug, or working with other sites), Sinai Health needs a written agreement before that activity can start. The team works with Sinai Health's Legal / Research Contracts office, which negotiates and signs agreements on the institution's behalf.

    AgreementUsed whenExamples from our work
    Clinical Trial Agreement (CTA)An industry- or externally sponsored trialTrials with a sponsor or supplied drug
    Data Sharing / Transfer Agreement (DSA/DTA)Data leaves or enters Sinai HealthMulti-centre consortia; registry data from BORN Ontario or CARTR Plus
    Material Transfer Agreement (MTA)Biological samples go to or come from another institutionSamples sent to external labs
    Research Collaboration AgreementSeveral sites share the work, funding or intellectual propertyMulti-site studies with SickKids, Princess Margaret and others
    Confidentiality Agreement (CDA/NDA)Confidential information is exchanged before a study startsEarly discussions with industry
    Service agreementAn outside vendor provides a paid serviceExternal testing or analysis

    9.2 The workflow

    1. Identify the need early, during study planning or when a change is proposed.
    2. Gather the details: the parties, scope, data or materials involved, budget, PI, REB status and timelines.
    3. Submit the request to Legal / Research Contracts.
    4. Relay questions between Legal, the PI and the external party, and keep a record of every exchange.
    5. The PI reviews the final draft, and then Sinai Health signs. A PI can't sign on the institution's behalf.
    6. File the signed agreement on the shared drive and record it in the tracker, with any expiry date.
    7. Track amendments and renewals, such as extensions and scope changes.

    9.3 Ground rules

    • No data or samples leave Sinai Health until both the agreement is signed and the REB has approved.
    • Follow up on pending agreements every 1–2 weeks. Contract negotiations stall easily.
    • Keep an agreements log (study, agreement type, other party, status, date submitted, date signed, expiry). This becomes part of the tracker upgrade.

    Systems and data

    10.1 eIVF (the clinic's electronic medical record)

    What you use it for: receiving referral tasks, adding study flowsheet labels, writing progress notes about enrolment and study status, and checking where a patient is in their treatment cycle so you contact them at the right time.

    Rules: open only the charts you need for your studies; eIVF keeps an audit trail of every chart you open. Always use the approved label or note wording for each study.

    10.2 REDCap

    • What you use it for: study databases, e-consent, patient surveys and follow-up questionnaires.
    • Project lifecycle: development → testing → production (needs the PI's approval). When a study ends, the project is set to read-only or archived. Transfer and ownership requests go to the REDCap team.
    • User rights: give each person the minimum access they need, and remove access as soon as someone leaves a study.
    • Good practice: keep a data dictionary, test every new instrument with dummy records before going live, and never change a production form without checking whether the REB needs to approve the change.

    10.3 Simple Survey

    Used for patient-facing questionnaires and QI surveys. Before launching a survey, confirm whether it's anonymous or identifiable, and make sure it matches the REB-approved version.

    10.4 Registries: CARTR Plus and BORN Ontario

    CARTR Plus is Canada's national registry of assisted reproduction cycles. BORN Ontario is the province's registry of pregnancy, birth and child outcomes. Some studies use registry data under agreements (Section 9); you'll help coordinate data requests and the secure SFTP transfers, which IT sets up.

    10.5 NVivo

    Used to analyse qualitative data, such as interview and focus-group transcripts. Keep project files on the secure research drive, and only load de-identified transcripts.

    10.6 The shared research drive

    X:\IVF\Research holds study folders (grouped by PI and study), the REB MasterFile, templates, training records, and the "Inactive OR Completed studies" archive. Save files in the right study folder, with the version-control naming convention (Section 7). Never keep study files on your desktop or a personal device.

    10.7 Data handling essentials

    • Keep identifiable information to a minimum. Use study IDs on data forms, and store the key linking study IDs to patients separately, with restricted access.
    • Use only secure channels: Sinai Health email, the MSFResearch mailbox and approved platforms. Never use personal email, personal cloud storage or unencrypted USB drives.
    • Report any suspected privacy breach the same day to Swati, the PI and the Sinai Health Privacy Office.
    • Keep records for as long as required by the protocol and Sinai Health policy. Clinical trials regulated by Health Canada have longer retention periods.

    Glossary

    Clinical terms

    AMH
    Anti-Müllerian hormone; a blood test of ovarian reserve.
    AFC
    Antral follicle count; an ultrasound measure of ovarian reserve.
    COS
    Controlled ovarian stimulation: daily hormone injections to grow several follicles.
    FSH / LH
    Follicle-stimulating and luteinizing hormones; Gonal-F (FSH) and Luveris (LH) are stimulation drugs.
    Trigger
    The injection that finalizes egg maturation about 36 hours before retrieval.
    IUI
    Intrauterine insemination.
    IVF / ICSI
    In vitro fertilization; intracytoplasmic sperm injection (one sperm injected into each egg).
    MI / MII
    Immature (metaphase I) and mature (metaphase II) eggs.
    Blastocyst
    An embryo at day 5–7 of development.
    Vitrification
    Rapid freezing of eggs or embryos.
    FET
    Frozen embryo transfer.
    PGT-A
    Preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy (euploid = normal chromosome number; aneuploid = abnormal).
    OHSS
    Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome, a complication of stimulation.
    RPL
    Recurrent pregnancy loss.
    MVA
    Manual vacuum aspiration, used to manage early pregnancy loss.
    NP
    Nurse practitioner.

    Research and admin terms

    REB
    Research Ethics Board.
    SHORES
    The Sinai Health REB's online submission system.
    TCPS 2
    Tri-Council Policy Statement on ethical conduct for research involving humans.
    GCP
    Good Clinical Practice (ICH E6), the standard for running clinical trials.
    Division 5
    The part of Canada's Food and Drug Regulations covering drug clinical trials.
    PHIPA
    Ontario's Personal Health Information Protection Act.
    CTO
    Clinical Trials Ontario, which runs single ethics review for multi-site studies.
    RCT
    Randomized controlled trial.
    QI
    Quality improvement.
    Delegation log
    The record of which team members are authorized for which study tasks.
    Deviation
    Any departure from the approved protocol.
    IO
    Internal order (cost centre) holding a study's funds.
    Float
    A cash advance for participant reimbursements.
    CTA / DSA / MTA
    Clinical trial, data-sharing and material-transfer agreements.
    TDG
    Transportation of Dangerous Goods (shipping biological specimens).
    eIVF
    The clinic's electronic medical record.
    REDCap
    Secure web platform for study databases and surveys.
    CARTR Plus / BORN
    National assisted-reproduction registry / Ontario's birth outcomes registry.
